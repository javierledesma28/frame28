## What you'll learn

- Which moments of your video become shorts and why.
- The ten hook types and how we pull them from your transcript.
- The thirty-second arc and where the call to action goes.

## Script outline

1. **Hook.** "Three seconds. That's what you have to make someone stay. And you decide it with text, not voice."
2. **What a moment is.** Result ("and that's it"), promise ("beginner friendly"), objection ("what if it cracks?"),
   number, product. A 15-to-45-second stretch with several moments is a short.
3. **How segments are chosen.** They start and end on a full sentence; they score higher if they open with pain or
   promise and end on a result.
4. **The ten hook types**, with an on-screen example of each.
5. **Where they come from.** The transcript: result sentence, objection, literal promise. Never invented figures.
6. **Why two hooks per short.** Platforms burn a creative in one or two weeks; you rotate the opening, not the body.
7. **The thirty-second arc.** Hook, promise, steps, result, social proof, action.
8. **The call to action.** Real price, code, "link in bio" or QR depending on where it's watched. Never before
   showing the result.
9. **What happens if your video has no moments.** We tell you: what to shoot next time.
10. **Closing.** Homework and lesson five.

## Key lines

"Three seconds." · "Text, not voice." · "Two hooks, one body." · "Rotate the opening." · "The action comes after the result."

## What the edit puts on screen

An example short with the arc marked by timings; the ten types as a focus list; two variants of the same short side
by side; a call-to-action card with price, code and QR.

## Homework

Write three hooks for your video, one transformation, one pain and one benefit, using sentences you actually say in
it. Two lines, five words per line.
