How to shoot so it comes out right

Ten shooting rules for a person talking to camera. The better the footage, the more the edit can do.

The edit adds titles, text synced to your voice, text behind you, callouts where you point and on-screen steps. All of that depends on how the clip was shot. You don't need professional gear: you need discipline on ten things.

The ten rules

  1. Fixed camera, fixed background. A tripod or a stable surface. No panning, no zoom. The text that appears behind you and the cutout of your silhouette depend on the background not moving.
  2. Plenty of light from the front. A window in front of you, or a lamp. Low light causes motion blur when you move, and hands come out semi-transparent when we put text behind you.
  3. Framing with air. Center yourself or sit on a third, leaving free space on one side for the text. Head below the top third. Nothing bright or high-contrast right behind your head.
  4. Mic close to your mouth. A lapel mic or your earphones' mic works. Record even if it sounds quiet (we normalize it), but with no background noise: air conditioning, street, music.
  5. Short sentences, one idea each. A brief pause between ideas. That way each sentence becomes a clean subtitle and the cuts land where they should.
  6. Useful, slow gestures. If you point to one side when you name something, we put a callout there. The gesture has to last half a second and hold still for a moment.
  7. Tell us the proper nouns. Brands, products, people, acronyms. Speech recognition confuses them; with the list they come out right first time.
  8. One take per chapter. Change position or shirt between blocks: it marks the chapter change without transitions and gives us a natural place to cut.
  9. 1080p at 30 fps is enough. 4K or 60 fps don't improve the result and double the processing time. Horizontal if it goes to YouTube; we make the vertical versions from the horizontal.
  10. No prior editing. No cuts, text, music or logos already baked in. The edit adds all of that; if it's already there, it can't be moved or translated.

Before you press record

  • Do a ten-second test and watch it on your phone: can you hear yourself? Is there space on one side? Is the light even?
  • Keep the script or bullet points at hand, out of frame. Looking at the camera matters more than saying it perfectly.
  • Leave two seconds of silence at the start and end of each take.

If the video already exists

Voice-over tutorials, overhead shots of hands, creator clips: they work too. We detect the text already on screen so we never cover it, and place ours in the free areas. We only ask for the original file at the highest quality you have.
