Formats and safe zones per platform

Which format goes where, what the phone's interface covers, and why a video isn't reused across formats without repositioning the text.

Three canvases

Canvas Where What changes
16:9 (1920×1080) YouTube, website, email, presentations the speaker on one side and the text on the other; subtitles at the bottom
9:16 (1080×1920) TikTok, Reels, Shorts the speaker fills the center; text at the top; word-by-word subtitles over the chest
1:1 (1080×1080) product page, Amazon, square Instagram no social hook; steps and result; no "link in bio"

A video isn't reused across canvases by stretching or cropping: every text is repositioned. That's why we make the vertical from the horizontal following the speaker (a window that moves with the face, like a camera operator) or, when there are hands and product, with the whole video over a blurred background that leaves two free bands at the top and bottom for the text.

What the phone covers in vertical

Apps draw their interface on top of the video. There are three zones where nothing important should land:

  • Right column: the like, comment and share icons, from 35 % to 88 % of the height.
  • Bottom band: the account name, the description and the progress bar, the bottom 16 %.
  • Top band: the phone's status and the tabs, the top 8 %.

The edit checks this by itself: if a text lands in one of those zones, it warns and gets moved. Word-by-word subtitles sit above the bottom 16 %.

Subtitles: by sentence or by words

  • By sentence (YouTube, website): a box at the bottom, two lines at most, 42 characters per line, between one and seven seconds each, no overlaps. It's what the SRT file exports.
  • By words (vertical): groups of two to four words that appear as they're said, in capitals, Shorts style. Or karaoke: the sentence visible and the current word in your accent color.

Durations that work

  • Short: between 15 and 45 seconds; 30 is the target. It starts and ends on a full sentence, never mid-word.
  • Long video: as long as the content needs; from five minutes we treat it as two pieces.
  • Welcome video ("new here?"): 20 to 35 seconds, aimed at "start today".

Cover

The YouTube thumbnail carries a frame of you, the title in two lines with highlight and your logo. If the video has hook variants, each variant can have its cover with the same hook.
