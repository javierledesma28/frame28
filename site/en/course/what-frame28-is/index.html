What Frame28 is and what you'll receive

The opening lesson. What comes out of a video, what the process looks like and what's on you. Eight minutes.

Video in preparation: shot to camera and edited with Frame28. The script is below in the meantime.

What you'll learn

  • Which five pieces come out of a single video and what each one is for.
  • The three steps of the process and where you come in.
  • How to order so the first delivery lands first time.

Script (to shoot to camera)

Hook (0–3 s). You send a video. And you get the whole campaign. I'll show you what that means in eight minutes.

Who I am (3–20 s). I'm Javier, from Think28. Frame28 is what we built so a brand with good videos stops losing half of what it shoots. This course is shot to camera, like any clip of yours, and edited with Frame28. What you see on screen is what you'll receive.

The problem (20 s–1:10). You shoot a good tutorial. You upload it to YouTube. And there it stays. No shorts, because cutting and adding text takes an afternoon. No decent cover, because you'd need another program. No German version, even though you sell in Germany. And the product page still has the same old photo. It isn't a lack of will: it's that every piece is a different job. Frame28 makes them the same job.

The five pieces (1:10–3:00). Five things come out of one video. One: the long video edited with your brand. Titles, numbered steps, the number you say on screen, the callout where you point, the before and after, the social proof and the closing with your logo. Two: vertical shorts. Each with two different hooks and the same body, to rotate the opening when the first one wears out. Three: the YouTube cover and the square version for the product page. Four: the subtitles as a file, because seven out of ten people watch with the sound off. And five: the version in another language, with text and subtitles translated and the same timing. All with your brand, because we configure it once and it comes out on its own.

The process (3:00–5:00). Three steps. First, you send us the video and the brief. The video, a link or a file. The brief, ten questions: what you sell, the product, your promises in your words, the social proof, the closing, colors, font, logo, tone and what you don't want. Fifteen minutes, once. Second, we direct it and edit it by code. We read the whole video: what's said, when, where you point, which sentence sells. From that we write an edit script and every piece comes out of it. No templates: every text is decided for that video. Third, you get a contact sheet and the package. The contact sheet is an image with a frame from every piece every few seconds. You look at it in a minute, ask for your round of changes in one list, and publish.

What's on you (5:00–6:30). Three things, and all three are easy. Shoot with a fixed camera, light from the front and air on one side: lesson two. Answer the brief in your words, not ours: lesson three. And review the contact sheet checking figures, prices and names, which are yours: lesson five. If you do those three things, the round of changes is usually one line.

Deadlines and guarantee (6:30–7:20). Launch is five business days from the moment we have video, brief and logo. If in thirty days you haven't published at least half of the pieces, we refund it. Not because we expect to refund: because the production cost is low and the fear of trying is high, and we'd rather remove the fear.

Closing (7:20–8:00). Next lesson: how to shoot so all of this works. Ten rules, none needs professional gear. And if you already have a video, even an edited one, it works too: we build on top. See you in lesson two.

What the edit puts on screen

Kinetic titles with the five pieces as they're named; the three steps as a board; a "7 out of 10" counter at the sound-off line; a card with "5 business days" and "30-day guarantee"; closing with the logo and the website.

Homework

Pick the video you want to start with and write in one line what it sells. You'll use it in lesson three.
